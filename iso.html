Before you buy anything from anyone

Two editions, two clocks

For the first time the two most widely held ISO standards have separated. If you run an integrated management system you are now facing two transitions on two different timetables, and planning them as one programme costs far less than paying for each in turn.

ISO 14001 has already moved

The 2026 edition published in April 2026 and the 2015 edition was withdrawn on 15 April 2026. Certification bodies stop issuing new certificates to the 2015 edition on 31 October 2027, and every organisation must have transitioned by 30 April 2029.

ISO 9001 has not

The sixth edition is still under publication, with ISO indicating September 2026. Its transition has not formally started. The changes are evolutionary rather than structural, so there is no reason to delay certifying to the 2015 edition now.

Everything else is unchanged

Clauses 4 to 10 are shared across 9001, 14001, 45001 and 22301, so a second standard is far less work than the first. Each pack states the edition its documents are written against.

Where organisations get caught out

Assuming you have until 2029 to start

You have until April 2029 to finish. Certification bodies stop issuing to the 2015 edition in October 2027, and audit slots in the months before any deadline are scarce. Treat 2027 as the real date.

A certificate that is not UKAS-accredited

Cheap, quick, and rejected by exactly the supply chain audits you bought it for. Verify any supplier's certificate on UKAS CertCheck, and expect your own customers to do the same to you.

Treating the climate amendment as paperwork

Amendment 1:2024 requires you to decide whether climate change is a relevant issue in your context, and to show that you decided. It does not require emissions data. But "not relevant" with no reasoning behind it is a finding.

What it is

ISO 14001 specifies requirements for an environmental management system. It asks you to work out which of your activities interact with the environment, which of those interactions matter, what obligations apply, and what you are doing about all three, then to show that the system improves rather than merely persists.

The 2026 edition is an evolution rather than a rebuild. The management system skeleton is unchanged; what has moved is the breadth of what counts as environmental context, and the emphasis on demonstrable performance rather than maintained process.

Key facts

Current edition
ISO 14001:2026 — Environmental management systems — Requirements with guidance for use
Published
April 2026, replacing ISO 14001:2015
Status of 2015
Withdrawn on 15 April 2026
Transition
New certificates to the 2015 edition stop 31 October 2027; all organisations must transition by 30 April 2029
Certifiable
Yes, by a UKAS-accredited certification body

Who needs it

It is rarely a standalone decision. Most organisations arrive at it through one of three routes:

  • Manufacturers and processors with permits or consents. Regulatory exposure is real and a systematic approach is cheaper than reacting to enforcement.
  • Suppliers to large corporates and the public sector. Scope 3 reporting has pushed environmental questions down the supply chain, and buyers increasingly want a certificate rather than a questionnaire.
  • Organisations that already hold ISO 9001. The shared structure makes 14001 the cheapest second certification to add, and joint audits cost less than two separate ones.

Where organisations get caught out

Assuming you have until 2029 to start

You have until 30 April 2029 to finish. Certification bodies stop issuing to the 2015 edition on 31 October 2027, and audit slots in the months before a deadline are always scarce. Treat 2027 as the real date.

An aspects register that lists activities rather than aspects

"Vehicle fleet" is an activity. The aspect is fuel consumption, and the impact is resource depletion and emissions. Registers built at the wrong level of granularity produce significance scores nobody can defend.

No evidence for the new clause 6.3

A change register driven by aspects is not the same as planning changes to the management system itself. If nothing in your documentation shows the four considerations 6.3 requires, expect a finding.

What it is

ISO/IEC 27001 specifies requirements for an Information Security Management System, an ISMS. It is not a technical checklist of security products. It is a governance framework: how you decide what information matters, what could go wrong, which controls you will apply, and how you prove any of it is working.

That framing catches people out. Organisations often arrive expecting a list of things to install and find instead a requirement to make and evidence decisions. The controls matter, but the auditable substance is the decision-making around them.

Key facts

Published
25 October 2022 (third edition), plus Amendment 1:2024 on climate action
Certifiable
Yes. It is the most widely recognised security certification in procurement

Any organisation holding information worth protecting, at any size, in any sector

The deadline to move from the 2013 edition passed on 31 October 2025

Who needs it

In practice, most organisations pursue 27001 because someone made them:

  • Enterprise and public-sector procurement. The most common trigger by a distance. Certification moves from nice-to-have to deal-blocker the moment you sell upmarket.
  • Handling other organisations' data. If you are a processor, your clients' own compliance obligations flow down to you through contract.
  • Regulated or high-trust sectors such as finance, health, legal and critical infrastructure, where demonstrable security governance is expected rather than requested.

If your certificate still says 2013, it is no longer valid

The transition period to the 2022 edition ended on 31 October 2025. Certificates against ISO/IEC 27001:2013 were withdrawn at that point. They did not quietly run to their expiry date.

If you missed it, there is no transition route left. You return through a full initial certification: Stage 1 and Stage 2 audits as though you had never been certified. The practical consequence is a gap in your certification history, which procurement teams do notice. Worth checking the date on your certificate before a client does.

What it is

ISO 45001 sets out what an occupational health and safety management system has to do. It replaced OHSAS 18001, which was withdrawn in March 2021, and it is the only OH&S standard that carries genuine international accredited recognition.

Its defining feature is clause 5.4, consultation and participation of workers. Not a safety committee of managers; the standard specifically requires the participation of non-managerial workers and the removal of the barriers that stop them taking part: time, language, literacy, and fear of reprisal. Auditors ask to interview shop-floor staff directly, and that single clause is where most weak systems come apart.

It is not a legal requirement in Great Britain. Your duties sit in the Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 and the Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999. ISO 45001 is how you evidence that you have discharged them.

Key facts

Current edition
ISO 45001:2018 — Occupational health and safety management systems (1st edition, 12 March 2018, 41 pages)
Next edition
ISO/DIS 45001. The DIS ballot ran 16 June to 8 September 2026 and publication is targeted for 2027
Amendment
Amendment 1:2024 on climate action applies: one sentence at 4.1, one note at 4.2
Certifiable
Yes, UKAS-accredited, and inside the IAF Multilateral Recognition Arrangement since 5 March 2020
Structure
Harmonized Structure, clauses 4 to 10, shared with 9001, 14001 and 22301

Who needs it

The driver is almost always a customer or an insurer rather than a regulator:

  • Construction and civils subcontractors bidding to Tier 1 main contractors. Under the Common Assessment Standard Question Set version 5, live since 1 July 2025, Question 71 exempts you from the entire health-and-safety question block if you hold either a valid SSIP certificate or ISO 45001 from a UKAS-accredited body. That is a hard tender gate, not a nice-to-have.
  • Facilities management, highways and social housing contractors. Constructionline Gold and Platinum and CHAS Elite sit on top of the Common Assessment Standard and are routinely written into framework prequalification.
  • High-hazard manufacturing, waste, logistics and utilities. Employers' liability premium negotiation and customer supply-chain audits, plus demonstrable "adequate arrangements" if the HSE ever investigates you.

Where organisations get caught out

A safety committee of managers presented as worker consultation

This breaches clause 5.4 b) to d) directly. The standard requires emphasis on the participation of non-managerial workers and the removal of the barriers that prevent it. An auditor will ask to speak to operatives without a manager present, and what they say is the evidence.

Contractors managed with a signing-in book and an insurance certificate

Clause 8.1.4.2 requires you to coordinate your OH&S management system with your contractors' and to apply OH&S criteria to their selection. Where activity is outsourced, 8.1.4.3 applies as well. This is where the majority of major nonconformities land.

Risk registers that jump straight to PPE and training

Clause 8.1.2 requires the hierarchy of controls to be applied in order: elimination, substitution, engineering controls, administrative controls, then PPE. Auditors expect to see that elimination and substitution were demonstrably considered and why they were rejected, not simply that hard hats were issued.

What it is

ISO 22301 specifies requirements for a business continuity management system: how you work out what would hurt, how quickly it would hurt, what you will do about it, and how you prove the plan works before you need it.

It is the shortest of the major management system standards at twenty-one pages, and the most operationally demanding, because clause 8 asks for artefacts most organisations do not have: a business impact analysis derived from impact over time rather than from IT's recovery estimates, costed resource requirements, a response structure with defined authority to invoke, and an exercise programme that actually runs.

Almost everyone who fails in a real incident had a plan. What they lacked was tested resource provisioning and someone with the authority to declare.

Key facts

Current edition
ISO 22301:2019 — Security and resilience — Business continuity management systems (2nd edition, 30 October 2019, 21 pages)
Next edition
ISO/CD 22301, third edition. The committee draft comment period closed 10 May 2026 and no DIS ballot has opened.
Amendment
Amendment 1:2024 on climate action applies
Certifiable
Yes, and UKAS accredits it, though business continuity is not a sub-scope of the IAF Multilateral Recognition Arrangement
Structure
Harmonized Structure, clauses 4 to 10, with almost everything distinctive inside clause 8

Who needs it

Three groups, and for the first the driver is a regulator that ISO 22301 does not satisfy:

  • FCA- and PRA-regulated firms, payment and e-money institutions, and qualifying cryptoasset firms. The PS21/3 operational resilience regime. The transition ended on 31 March 2025; PS7/26, published 18 March 2026, adds operational incident reporting within 24 hours and material third-party registers, effective 18 March 2027.
  • Cloud, SaaS, BPO and managed service suppliers to UK financial services. The critical third parties regime went live on 13 July 2026 with AWS, Google Cloud, Microsoft and Oracle designated. Everyone below that threshold now absorbs the diligence burden as a "material third party", and ISO 22301 is the artefact buyers ask for.
  • Data centres, telecoms, logistics and outsourced service providers. Contractual mandate in supplier resilience schedules, rather than statute. It appears in the schedule, not the specification, which is why it is often discovered late.

Where organisations get caught out

A business impact analysis built from IT's recovery time objectives

This breaches 8.2.2 c) and d). The process must assess impacts over time against defined impact types and criteria, and from that identify the point at which impact becomes unacceptable. RTO is a downstream note, not the input. Working backwards from what IT can currently deliver produces a BIA that documents your constraints rather than your requirements.

Continuity strategies chosen without costing the resources to deliver them

Clause 8.3.4 requires each resource category to be determined. "Relocate to Site B" with no seat count, no data replication arrangement and no supplier standby contract fails here, and it fails in the incident too.

An annual desktop exercise treated as satisfying the whole of clause 8

Clause 8.6 is a separate requirement from 8.5 exercising and from 9.2 internal audit. You must periodically evaluate the suitability, adequacy and effectiveness of the BIA, risk assessment, strategies, solutions and plans, and evaluate them again after every disruption, test and exercise. It is the most commonly missed clause in the standard.

What it is

ISO 13485 specifies quality management system requirements for organisations involved in the life cycle of a medical device. The clue is in the subtitle: requirements for regulatory purposes. It exists to satisfy regulators, not customers, which is why it deliberately omits ISO 9001's customer satisfaction and continual improvement obligations in favour of maintaining effectiveness and meeting regulatory requirements.

The single most-missed fact about it is structural. ISO 13485 has clauses 4 to 8, not 4 to 10. It follows the ISO 9001:2008 architecture. There is no clause 4.1 context of the organisation, no 6.1 risks and opportunities, no clause 9 and no clause 10. It still carries a named management representative at 5.5.2, the role ISO 9001 deleted in 2015.

That is why integrating ISO 13485 with ISO 9001, 14001 or 45001 is genuinely harder than integrating any two Annex SL standards. Annex B of the 2016 edition maps the two if you need to run a combined system.

Key facts

Current edition
ISO 13485:2016 — Medical devices — Quality management systems — Requirements for regulatory purposes (3rd edition, 1 March 2016, 36 pages)
Revision
None. ISO/TC 210 reviewed and confirmed the 2016 edition on 31 October 2025, a deliberate decision not to revise
Amendment
Amendment 1:2024 on climate action does not apply. ISO 13485 is not a Harmonized Structure standard
Certifiable
Yes, UKAS-accredited and inside the IAF MLA. Separate from UK Approved Body designation for UKCA marking
Structure
Clauses 4 to 8, based on ISO 9001:2008, rather than the clause 4 to 10 Harmonized Structure

Who needs it

Three groups, and for two of them the driver is a national regulator rather than a customer:

  • GB manufacturers, own-brand labellers and contract manufacturers. UK MDR 2002 and UKCA marking via a UK Approved Body. MHRA notes that using standards is not mandatory, but in practice no UK Approved Body will grant a QMS certificate against anything else.
  • UK and EU manufacturers exporting to the United States. The FDA's Quality Management System Regulation took effect on 2 February 2026 and incorporates ISO 13485:2016 by reference into 21 CFR Part 820. This is the largest change in US device quality regulation in three decades.
  • Suppliers into NHS trusts and into EU MDR/IVDR conformity assessment. EU MDR Annex IX requires a full QMS audited by a Notified Body, for which EN ISO 13485 is the de facto route. GB recognition of CE-marked devices runs to 30 June 2028 or 2030 depending on the route.

Where organisations get caught out

Treating the medical device file as the EU technical documentation, or keeping one file for the whole portfolio

Clause 4.2.3 requires a file, or a reference set, for each medical device type or family: general description, intended use, labelling, specifications, manufacturing and measuring procedures, and installation and servicing requirements. Auditors pick a device and ask for its file.

Spreadsheets, eQMS modules, ERP and test software running unvalidated

Clause 7.5.6 covers validation of computer software used in production and service provision; clause 4.1.6 covers software used in the quality management system itself. Each requires a documented, risk-proportionate approach with records. This is the most common ISO 13485 major nonconformity, and since February 2026 it is a QMSR exposure as well.

Complaint handling and vigilance run as one undifferentiated customer feedback process

Clause 8.2.2 requires a documented complaint-handling procedure with defined timeliness, investigation, and a recorded rationale where no investigation is performed. Clause 8.2.3 separately requires a documented procedure for notifying regulatory authorities and issuing advisory notices. Under the GB post-market surveillance regulations in force since 16 June 2025, conflating them is a regulatory problem rather than an audit finding.

How the implementation guide works

The implementation guide is how the document packs become a fully fledged management system, rather than just a downloaded folder of templates. Every document in the pack is broken down into the same five part structure:

01

What it is for

The clause it answers to, who owns it, and why the document exists, including the failure it is there to prevent. Not a description of the file: an account of the risk.

02

How it is used

Which section does what, which fields are deliberately left blank for you to set, and which part is the diagnostic. Written so you can hand it to somebody else.

03

What good looks like

The way this is done well, and the specific way it usually goes wrong. This is the part that reads like a consultant rather than a template, because it names the failure most services do not know they have.

04

The process around it

A numbered sequence for putting it in place. Who reads what, who agrees it, where it gets minuted, and what has to be notified and by when.

05

What it connects to

The other documents it depends on and feeds, each with the reason for the link. This is how the pack behaves as a system instead of forty unrelated files.

Watch for new editions

Standards are revised, and a superseded edition is the fastest way to fail a recertification audit. Each pack states the edition it is written against.

What we will not tell you

That buying documents makes you certified. Certification comes from an accredited body after an audit, and no template pack changes that. What these documents do is remove the blank page, so the work you take into the audit is your own evidence rather than your own formatting.

Not sure which standard applies?

Most firms arrive because a customer asked for one by name, and that is usually the right answer. Where it gets harder is the overlap: information security and AI management share a great deal but answer different questions, and quality and environmental are routinely run as one system rather than two. If you already hold one certificate, the clause structure is the same from clause 4 to clause 10, so a second standard is far less work than the first.
