Before you buy anything from anyone

CQC compliance is not a certification

It is statutory registration by a public regulator with prosecution powers, against the 14 regulated activities in Schedule 1 of the Regulated Activities Regulations 2014, with legally binding conditions attached to each registration.

There is no certificate

No accredited body, nothing to renew, and no consultant who can register you. Advisers can prepare you; only CQC registers you. Anyone offering CQC certification is describing something that does not exist.

The law is not what is changing

The Regulated Activities Regulations 2014 and the fundamental standards at Regulations 8 to 20A are unchanged, and so are the five key questions. What is being rebuilt is how CQC organises its questions and reaches a rating.

Operating unregistered is a criminal offence

Each of the 14 regulated activities needs its own registration. A personal care agency that starts offering nursing, or a clinic registered for treatment that starts offering diagnostic imaging, has committed an offence under section 10. It is CQC's most straightforward prosecution.

Where providers get caught out

An evidence library indexed to the 34 quality statements

Providers who spent 2024 and 2025 building a folder per quality statement hold an index that will not survive the transition. The regulations do not change; the assessment lens does. Index evidence to the regulation and the key question and the mapping survives whatever comes next. Every pack here is built that way.

Adding a service without varying registration

The most common way an established provider ends up in front of CQC for something other than care quality. Check what you are registered for against what you actually do, on the same day each year.

Buying a promise nobody can make

No one can guarantee a rating, and you should be wary of anyone implying otherwise. CQC is a statutory regulator making its own judgements, and the incoming method moves deliberately toward professional judgement and away from a scoreable checklist.

Where the framework change actually stands

The Single Assessment Framework is still the operative framework, and you are still assessed under it. Anyone telling you it has been abolished is wrong.

Four draft sector-specific frameworks published24 March 2026
Consultation on the drafts closed12 June 2026
Pilots running alongside normal inspectionsSummer 2026
Implementation beginsTowards the end of 2026

What changes is how CQC organises its questions and reaches a rating: quality statements out, key lines of enquiry in, numerical scoring out, rating characteristics back. The law does not move. Your governance, safeguarding, staffing and audit evidence carries over. An index built against the 34 quality statements does not.

How the implementation guide works

The implementation guide is how the document packs become a fully fledged management system, rather than just a downloaded folder of templates. Every document in the pack is broken down into the same five part structure:

01

What it is for

The regulation it answers to, who owns it, and why the document exists, including the failure it is there to prevent. Not a description of the file: an account of the risk.

02

How it is used

Which section does what, which fields are deliberately left blank for you to set, and which part is the diagnostic. Written so you can hand it to somebody else.

03

What good looks like

The way this is done well, and the specific way it usually goes wrong. This is the part that reads like a consultant rather than a template, because it names the failure most services do not know they have.

04

The process around it

A numbered sequence for putting it in place. Who reads what, who agrees it, where it gets minuted, and what has to be notified and by when.

05

What it connects to

The other documents it depends on and feeds, each with the reason for the link. This is how the pack behaves as a system instead of fifty unrelated files.

What we will not tell you

That buying documents makes you certified. Certification comes from an accredited body after an audit, and no template pack changes that. What these documents do is remove the blank page, so the work you take into the audit is your own evidence rather than your own formatting.

Two packs that are easy to confuse

General practice and independent doctors may appear the same but are different in reality. General practice carries duties that come from an NHS primary medical services contract, the performers list and the Parliamentary and Health Service Ombudsman. A private clinic has none of those, and the exemption in Schedule 2 rests on who is on a performers list and who is not. Buy the wrong one and roughly a third of it does not apply to you, while the thing you most need is not in there.

If you work in NHS primary medical services, you should purchase general practice. If you do not, you should purchase independent doctors, clinics and community. The free assessment asks about the regulated activities you carry on and tells you which, and it will say so if you need both.
