The ask

Why this is free

The full Standard, all fourteen domains, the implementation guide and the twenty-two document templates and trackers are all available for free. No account, no email address, no payment, no trial that ends, no certification bodies that charge thousands for you to receive a certificate.

We felt it would be wrong to charge charities for a wholly voluntary, uncertifiable standard created around helping you to do more of what you already do best: helping society and making a real, tangible difference in the world.

What we ask instead

If the Standard is useful to you, please pay it forward and give £25 to a different charity that you care about.

Three things regarding this, so it is clear what is and is not happening.

The money does not come to us

We take nothing and we do not pass anything on. You choose the charity, you give directly, and the whole transaction has nothing to do with Sentinel.

No checks

There is no receipt to upload, no confirmation step, and no locked download waiting for proof. We will never know whether you gave anything. A Standard whose central idea is honest self-assessment would look ridiculous demanding evidence at the door.

It is not a condition

If money is tight, or the £25 is needed somewhere more urgent, take the Standard and use it. The organisations that most need a governance framework are usually the ones least able to pay for one. That is the whole reason it is free.

Why we are not doing this through a donation platform

We looked at routing payment through a giving platform, so that every download raised money for a named charity. It is a good idea and we may come back to it. Two things stopped us for now.

The law is specific about this

Under the Charities Act 1992, a business that sells something while representing that proceeds go to a charity is a commercial participator. That requires a written agreement with each charity before a single sale, and a statement at the point of sale saying exactly what proportion they receive. Those rules exist for good reasons and we are not going to work around them by being vague about where money goes.

We have not asked anyone yet

We have not asked any charity for their blessing. Naming charities on a sales page before speaking to them would be exactly the sort of thing this Standard tells organisations not to do.

If you are a charity reading this

If a charity reading this would like to talk about doing it properly, we would welcome that. Please contact us using the form at the bottom of the page and put Partnership in the heading.

The Standard

Read it here, in full.

Nothing is held back and nothing is behind a form. The reader below carries the complete Standard: the conditions your staff, beneficiaries and trustees are actually working in, the framework and its scoring model, all fourteen domains, and the implementation sections that follow.

  • Fourteen domains and 117 core requirements, each with a real-world test
  • A six-point maturity scale, scored against evidence rather than intention
  • Twenty-two templates and trackers, one or more for every domain
  • An implementation guide ordered by consequence, not by domain number

The document pack

Twenty-two templates, trackers and the implementation guide

Every domain has at least one working document behind it. They are ordinary Word and Excel files with nothing locked, nothing watermarked and no macros, so they can be renamed, rebranded and cut about to suit how your organisation already works.

What is in it

  • An implementation guide with an order of work, and what to do first
  • Policies and procedures for safeguarding, fundraising, data protection and environment
  • Registers and trackers for partners, incidents, pay gaps, carbon and eligibility
  • Calculators for full cost recovery, reserves and cash flow resilience

How to use it

  • Start with the implementation guide, not the domain you find most interesting
  • Complete the four documents in the first fortnight list before anything else
  • Replace every square-bracketed placeholder with your own detail
  • Delete the example rows once you have real entries of your own
